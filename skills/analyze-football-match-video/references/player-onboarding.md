# First-use player onboarding

Use this flow when no usable `references/player-profile.md` exists. The goal is to understand the player before interpreting the video, not to make them pre-analyze the match.

A profile is usable for longitudinal coaching when it contains at least: age or age group, dominant foot, primary role, the four physical self-ratings, usual competition format and intensity, perceived problem or development goal, and storage/update preferences. An older or partial profile remains valuable; ask only for its missing essentials and preserve all existing match evidence.

## Ask one grouped questionnaire

Ask all missing essentials together so the player can answer in one message. Adapt the wording to the user's language and keep optional questions clearly optional.

1. **Basic profile:** age or age group, dominant foot, football experience.
2. **Role:** usual position and side, secondary positions, current coach instructions or team role.
3. **Physical self-assessment:** short acceleration, longer/recovery speed, endurance, and strength/contact ability. Use a simple 1–5 scale and invite one sentence of context.
4. **Football self-assessment:** perceived strengths, problems already noticed, what they most want to improve, and longer-term goal.
5. **Competition context:** match format, level or type, perceived intensity from 1–5, typical duration and substitution rules, and team formation or style if known.
6. **Optional context:** relevant pain, injury, workload, or other constraint the player wants considered. Explain that video analysis cannot diagnose injury.
7. **Privacy preference:** permission to save the answers locally and permission to update the profile automatically after future reviews.

Accept “unknown” or “not sure.” If the player answers only part of the questionnaire, record the remaining fields as unknown and continue; do not start another compulsory interview round. Do not block analysis because the player cannot answer a technical question. For a minor, store only age group when exact age is unnecessary and avoid collecting school, address, or guardian information.

## Summarize and confirm

Convert the answers into a short player model:

- Current role and competition demands.
- Self-perceived physical and football profile.
- Two or three current development questions, not premature conclusions.
- The comparison context for future matches.

Show the summary at the start of the analysis and invite correction, but do not require a second confirmation turn before opening the video. Treat unanswered fields as unknown and let the player correct the model at any time.

Handle storage choices separately:

- If profile storage and automatic updates are approved, create the private profile and update it after each review.
- If profile storage is approved but automatic updates are declined, save onboarding facts and ask before writing each match result.
- If profile storage is declined, keep the model session-only and do not create the private file. Explain that future cross-match comparison will require the information again.

Do not imply that a submitted link was opened or verified before actually accessing it.

## Match-specific intake after onboarding

For the first and later matches, require only:

- Video link or uploaded file.
- Team colour plus shirt or bib number, or equivalent identity cues.

Ask for position, side, playing intervals, score context, or user notes only if they differ from the profile or materially help. Timestamps and self-written analysis are optional. If playing intervals are unknown, locate them from the footage rather than requiring the player to provide them.
